Guide

What each word, number and mark on the pages means. Retake Coach checks photo quality only: whether a fundus photo is good enough to grade, and if not, what to fix. It does not look for disease.

The result

Every photo gets one of three results, with one instruction under it.

Keep
Good enough to grade. Move on to the next photo. A kept photo can still carry a small note, such as "The aim is a little off centre; this photo is still usable": worth fixing next time, no need to retake.
Retake
Not good enough to grade. The instruction names what to fix (focus, aim, or something in the way) and, where it was measured, where on the photo. "Built from what was measured on this photo": the instruction is a standard sentence filled in with the positions and areas measured on this photo, not free text.
Not a fundus photo
The picture is unlike the fundus photos the detector learned from: a different camera mode, a widefield photo, or not an eye at all. Check the camera mode or the file. These pictures are left out of the coach's note and the visit report.

The three checks

Each photo is checked for the three usual reasons a fundus photo cannot be graded. They come from the DeepDRiD dataset's photo-quality labels, which the checks were trained on.

Focus
Sharp or Blurry. Blurry means the small vessels are not crisp: the camera was not focused on the retina, or the patient moved. Fix: refocus on the small vessels before the shot.
Aim
Centred or Off centre. Whether the photo is centred on the part of the retina this view should show: the macula or the optic disc (see the eye and the views). Fix: the instruction says which structure to move toward the middle, for example "Re-aim so the optic disc moves to the middle".
Obstruction
Clear view or Shadow or glare. Something covers part of the retina: a dark shadow (often an eyelid, lashes or a small pupil) or glare and haze (often a smudged lens or the wrong working distance). Fix for a dark shadow: ask the patient to open wide and centre the camera on the pupil. For glare or haze: wipe the lens and adjust the working distance.
(still gradable)
Next to a check on a kept photo: the problem was noticed, but the photo can still be graded.
(from the marked area)
Next to Obstruction: its score stayed under its line, but the blue area on the photo is larger than on almost any clear photo, so it is pointed out anyway. See marks on the photo.

Marks on the photo

The ring around the photo shows the three checks: Focus at the top, Aim on the right, Obstruction on the left. Marks on the photo itself show what was measured.

Blue area
Shown on retakes. Always when the obstruction check asks for the retake; on a retake for another reason, only when the area is larger than on 95 of 100 clear photos. The area around the macula is left out in that second case, because the macula is naturally darker. It marks small squares of the photo that look most unlike the same place on clear photos, measured with DINOv3.
Where
The blue area in words: what it looks like and where it is, for example "Dark shadow toward the upper left, about a quarter of the photo". Dark shadow: the marked area is darker than the rest of the retina. Glare or haze: brighter. Something in the way: neither clearly.
Positions
Where the optic disc and macula were found, in words, for example "the optic disc is near the centre; the macula is near the right edge". Shown when aim is off.

Numbers under "What the check saw"

Score
How strongly a check sees its problem, from 0 to 1. Higher means the problem is more likely. The bar shows the score.
Flagged from
The line a score must reach for the check to count the problem; the tick on the bar marks it. Each line was set on DeepDRiD validation photos, not on the photos used to report results.
Overall
"Good enough to grade" or "Not good enough to grade", from a fourth score trained on DeepDRiD's overall quality label. A retake is asked when it reaches its line ("retake from"); the three checks then say why.
Fundus photo, energy
How unlike ordinary fundus photos the picture is. Low means familiar. Above the limit shown (about 6.9) the picture is turned away as "Not a fundus photo". Held-out fundus photos scored about 0.3 to 6, widefield photos 17 to 54, and everyday pictures 75 to 210. This guard learned what fundus photos look like without any quality labels.
Time
"Checked in": the time for the three checks and the positions. "Located in": the extra time to mark where an obstruction is, on retakes.
Photo
Which eye, and which view, worked out from the photo itself. See the eye and the views.
Sample
On a sample photo: its name and the DeepDRiD dataset's own label for it, to compare with the result.

The eye and the views

Fundus photo
A photo of the back of the eye (the retina), taken through the pupil with a fundus camera.
Optic disc
The round, pale spot where the optic nerve and the blood vessels enter the eye.
Macula
The darker area at the centre of vision, about 2.5 disc diameters from the optic disc. Its centre is the fovea.
Disc diameter
The width of the optic disc, about 1.5 to 1.8 mm: the usual ruler for distances on the retina. Aim is judged in disc diameters, so it works the same on any camera or photo size.
The two views
Screening takes two photos of each eye: one centred on the macula and one centred on the optic disc. A photo's view is whichever of the two is nearer the centre. Aim is good when that structure is within 2 disc diameters of the centre and the other one is well inside the picture (within 3).
Right or left eye
From the layout: in a right eye the optic disc sits to the right of the macula on the photo (true for 94% of DeepDRiD test photos). Both must be found; otherwise the page says "Eye not measured".

The visit (One photo page)

A visit is every photo checked since New patient was last pressed. Retake Coach does not know who the patient is: it reads no names or IDs, so press New patient between patients.

This visit
The checklist of the four views a screening visit needs: right and left eye, each centred on the macula and on the disc. Kept: a photo of that view was kept. Retake needed: photos of that view were taken, none kept yet. Not yet: no photo of that view so far.
Count line
For example "1 of 4 views kept. 5 checked, 1 kept, 3 to retake, 1 not a fundus photo; 1 where the eye or field couldn't be measured". The last part counts fundus photos where the optic disc or the macula was not found, so the view is unknown and the photo does not fill a box in the checklist.
Coach's note
One short note for the technician about the whole visit, not the last photo: the most common problem, the habit that prevents it, and any view with no kept photo yet. The button says how many photos it covers, for example "Coach's note on all 5 photos". It is written when you press it; after another photo, press it again.
Line under the note
What the photos it covers were, for example "3 kept, 1 sent back, 1 not a regular fundus photo (left out of the note)", and who wrote it: Muse Spark and what the note cost, or "Standard summary" when Muse Spark is off or its daily cap is reached.
What Muse Spark was given
Open it to see the exact text sent to Muse Spark: counts in words and the standard advice. Never a photo. If the note contains a number that is not in that text, the standard summary is shown instead.
New patient
Starts the next visit: clears the screen, the checklist and the note. It is greyed out for a moment while a photo is still being checked. When the visit report is on, the visit just finished is added to it first.

Visit report

Each press of New patient adds one row for the visit just finished to a CSV file on the computer running Retake Coach (reports/visits.csv), for training and review: for example, whether aim retakes per visit are going down over the weeks. Download it from the link at the bottom of the One photo page. It is off on the public demo.

Counts only. No photos, file names, patient names or IDs, and no clock times: the date alone cannot be matched to an appointment.

Many photos page

For checking a whole session or folder at once. It does not group photos by patient, so its coach's note leaves out missing views.

Table columns
File: the file name. Result: keep, retake, not a fundus photo, or unreadable. What to fix: the instruction. Eye and Centred on: the measured eye and view. Checked: when the photo was checked.
Unreadable
The file is not a picture the check can open, or the check did not finish. Export the photo as a JPEG and try again.
Watching a folder
When the app is started with a folder to watch (for example the folder a camera exports to over Wi-Fi), each new photo that lands there is checked and appears at the top of the table.
Download CSV
The table as a spreadsheet file. Unlike the visit report, it contains the file names, so keep it where patient files are kept.
Clear list
Empties the table and starts over. It adds nothing to the visit report.

Models and privacy

Detector
The badge at the top. DINOv2 is a Meta image model; it turns each photo into features, and small checks trained on DeepDRiD read them. DINOv3, also from Meta, measures where an obstruction is (the blue area). Both run on the computer next to the camera, and photos stay on it.
Coach's note badge
Muse Spark, Meta's language model, reached through the Meta Model API, writes the coach's note from counts in words only. The badge shows how much of today's spending cap is used, or "standard" when there is no key or the cap is reached; the note is then a fixed summary of the same counts.
What leaves the computer
Only the coach's note request: counts in words, no photos, no file names. Everything else stays on the computer running Retake Coach, and what a visit holds is kept in memory until New patient or a restart.
